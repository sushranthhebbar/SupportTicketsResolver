Section 2

The problem

2.1 What CloudServe asked for

CloudServe sells cloud infrastructure and developer tooling to just over 200 corporate customers. Tickets reach its six agents by email, chat, comments on documentation pages and a user forum, and the team is not coping. Marcus Adeyemi reports a first reply time of 8 to 12 hours against a two-hour agreement, first contact resolution of 42% against an industry figure he puts at 65%, and customer satisfaction that has dropped to 3.2 out of 5. He asked for a chatbot that handles the easy tickets so that response times fall without new hires. He was equally clear about what would make the project fail: wrong answers, more work for his agents, worse service for enterprise customers, and decisions nobody can explain.

The brief gives four more voices, in interview transcripts. Each of them sees one part of the operation, and Table 1 sets out what they said and what they could not see.

2.2 What the data showed

The documentation is not where the gap is. In the 500 development tickets, 71.4% are labelled answerable from the existing articles. More than half of all tickets (56.2%) were escalated, and 49.1% of the escalated ones were answerable from documentation, which lines up with Daniel's "about half". CloudServe holds most of the answers but cannot put them in front of the right person quickly, and it loses time a second time when a ticket does need a specialist, because the hand-over carries no context.

The gap CloudServe asked for automation. The evidence supports help with finding and drafting: locate the article, draft a cited reply, say when there is nothing to go on, and pass on what has been tried. It also says the cost of a mistake is high, since the head of support himself lists a wrong answer as a way the project fails. Both points argue for an assistant whose work an agent checks, not one that writes to customers by itself.

Three things stayed out of scope from the start. The project does not rewrite the knowledge base, because retrieval already finds the right article for nearly every answerable ticket. It does not replace any agent. And it must not widen the service gap Ravi worries about, so every result in this report is also looked at by customer group (Section 8).
