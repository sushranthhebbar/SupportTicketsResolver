5. Architecture and design

5.1 The system

Figure 2 shows the path of a ticket. The assistant is a Python service with a command-line evaluation runner and a small web page for agents. A ticket from any of the four channels is first normalised into one format, with the customer fields held separately and the evaluation labels and historical outcomes kept out of anything the model sees. A language model then classifies it, returning an intent from the 22 categories in the data, an urgency, a confidence for each, and for the urgency a short quotation from the ticket as evidence. The router applies a fixed policy to the result. Tickets whose intent is security incident, compliance request, feature request or unclear request go to a specialist; the system retrieves the closest articles and prepares an escalation summary for them. For every other ticket the system retrieves passages, asks the model to draft a reply using only those passages, and runs guardrails over the ticket and over the draft. A ticket that passes becomes a draft waiting for an agent; one that fails, or has too little evidence, becomes a note to a human saying why. Every step writes a row to the decision log.

Retrieval uses the 29 supplied knowledge-base articles. Each is split into passages of about 800 characters with a 120-character overlap, embedded with the MiniLM sentence model and stored in a persistent vector index, and the five nearest passages are returned with their document identifiers. The drafting model sees only those passages and the ticket, is told that ticket text is untrusted data, and must place an inline citation such as [DOC-AUTH-004] after each claim. The agent's review page lists the drafts, shows each with its sources, and lets the agent edit, approve, reject or escalate; approving re-runs the citation and safety checks. Observed outcomes and agent feedback have their own store, and a dashboard summarises decisions, guardrail activations and outcomes by customer tier and fluency, with a plain statement that it makes no causal claim. The classifier and the drafter call an OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint, and the evaluation used the free tier of Groq running the Qwen 3.8 27-billion-parameter model. Prompts are versioned PR-02 for drafting and PR-03 for classification.

5.3 What the architecture does not do

It does not have a live intake: tickets enter through the evaluation runner, the scripts or the tests, and nothing accepts them from a real support inbox. It does not measure the time an agent spends. It does not rank or detect duplicate tickets, although I found that repeated texts are common (Section 9). And its single model provider is a point of failure whose effects I tested rather than eliminated (Section 8).
