9.2 What it taught me

I started from the idea that confidence and urgency would work as control signals: a score would say which tickets could be answered and urgency which needed a person. The data said otherwise. What the interviews actually asked for was help finding the right article and a draft an agent can check, so the real job was making the agent's review dependable, not deciding what to automate. I would also look at the data before writing a target. Counting the repeated-text groups in the first week (67 of 97 with conflicting urgency labels) and comparing against the always-"medium" baseline would have shown that an urgency target meant nothing, and a small calibration check on 50 tickets would have done the same for confidence. I did neither until the second week.

Several things I chose not to change are in the log with the reason. I did not relabel the conflicting urgency groups, because the hidden labels may conflict in the same way. I did not switch calibration on, because the gate filtered nothing. I did not rewrite the knowledge base, because retrieval already finds an article for 98% of answerable tickets. And I kept the revised classification prompt after its hypothesis failed.
